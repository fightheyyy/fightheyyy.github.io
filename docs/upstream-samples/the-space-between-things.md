Open a page you enjoy reading and look at the spaces instead of the words. There is probably more structure there than you first noticed.

The gap before a heading, the distance between rows, and the width beside a paragraph tell you which things belong together. They work before you consciously read a label.

## Establish a rhythm

Try three distances first: a small gap for related details, a medium gap for a new thought, and a generous gap for a new section. Their relationship matters more than any particular unit.

A caption should sit close to its figure. A new section should have enough separation that you recognize the change while scanning. Repeating those relationships makes a page easier to understand.

## Measure with actual words

Empty rectangles are forgiving. Real titles wrap. Dates vary in length. An author may have a longer name than you designed for.

Put those cases in the page before tuning the margins. Read an entire article at the intended width. If your eyes keep losing their place, the line length or line-height may need attention.

> Whitespace is not what remains after the layout. It is part of the layout.

## Let the page get simpler

Remove a border. Lower the weight of a label. Give a paragraph another few pixels of line-height. Small changes can make a page feel less demanding without making it less informative.

Keep a before-and-after screenshot. Your memory of the previous version is usually kinder than the pixels.
