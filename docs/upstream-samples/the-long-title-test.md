A short placeholder title makes every layout look composed. A real title reveals whether the composition can adapt.

## Write the awkward version

Use a title with a long word, a subtitle that wraps three times, and an author whose name is longer than the date. Look at the page on a narrow screen.

The correct response is usually to let the content take more vertical space. Cutting every line short can make an index harder to distinguish, especially when several articles begin with similar words.

## Watch the neighbors

A wrapping title should not collide with reading time or squeeze the date into an unusable column. On smaller screens, move supporting metadata below the title rather than trying to preserve the desktop table at any cost.

## Keep it as a fixture

Once the layout handles the awkward example, leave it in your sample content. The next typography change will have something honest to work against.
